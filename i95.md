# i95 - Fun Casual Game

The game i95 is a simple, fun, and easy side scrolling game that takes the player on a linear adventure down i95. 

## Design Pillars

Design pillars
Eight rules explain every mechanic in the app; a proposed feature that breaks one does not belong in this style.
1. Nothing can fail. No input is wrong, blocked or punished. Hitting an obstacle is the fun, not a mistake.
2. No reading, no narration. No text or voiceover in play. The studio cut voiceover after testing showed narrated kids went passive, while kids playing silently talked and invented their own stories (Kidscreen).
3. Choices express, never decide. Destination, luggage and vehicle change flavor and jokes. None changes difficulty or outcome.
4. Every touch answers. Any input gets an immediate sound and motion response, including touches on things that do nothing else.
5. The child owns the pace. No timers. Speed, stopping and lingering are entirely theirs.
6. Scaffold, don't instruct. Steps run in a fixed order, and when a child stalls, the next touchable object jiggles as a hint (Common Sense Media).
7. No extrinsic rewards. No scores, stars, coins or unlocks earned by play. Laughter and surprise are the payoff.
8. One small, complete story per trip. Leave, travel, arrive, greet. Arrival is a welcome, not a win, and the next trip starts fresh.

## Style
The file is i95-toy-diorama.svg. It’s plain vector, layered back to front, so it opens cleanly in Figma or Illustrator. The sign text calls for Overpass, a free Google Font that looks like real highway sign lettering. Install it, or the sign falls back to Arial.

Style rules (Feel free to adjust specifcs as needed)
Silhouettes stay true. Landmarks, vehicles and road furniture keep their real shapes and proportions. Cuteness comes from softening, never from exaggeration.
Rounded, no outlines. Buildings use a 6px corner radius, signs 8px and small parts 2–4px.
One light source, from the left. Each building gets a flat shade on its right 28%, either black at 7% or a darker sibling color for landmarks. It also gets a 3px white cap along the top edge at 45% for a slight bevel.
Soft contact shadows. A black ellipse at 12–16% sits under every vehicle and tree.
Windows. A 5×6px rounded grid on a 9×11 pitch. Most are white at 75%, and about 1 in 5 is lit warm.
Vehicles. Real types only: taxi, family SUV or minivan, semi. Squash each one to 96% width and 107% height and oversize the wheels by 12%. Add a darker lower band, one gloss stripe on the body and a glint on each window. No faces.
Depth. The far skyline is a single pale haze color with no detail. All the detail lives in the near layer.
Authenticity anchors. Keep the green guide sign with its white border, the Interstate shield, a yellow left edge line, white dashed lanes, a Jersey barrier, highway light poles and rooftop water towers.

## Drive
• Two control schemes run at once, with no mode switch (letter to parents):
    ◦ Zones: tap the right side to accelerate, the left side to brake.
    ◦ Direct: pick the vehicle up with a finger and drag it along.
• Touching high on the screen lifts the vehicle: a hop at low speed, flight at high speed. The sky is a legal route.
• One road. No lanes, no steering, no wrong turns.
• Speed belongs to the child, including a full stop to look around.
• Progress shows as roadside markers counting down from 10 to arrival (Common Sense Media).
• The car reacts: The cars lights and sounds respond to bumps, speed and air time.

## Roadside set pieces
• Same route skeleton every trip (phase 2: with a different mix of set pieces each time)
• Real-trip sights: realistic crossing sign, billboards, stoplight, tunnel, ramp, river, bridge, mud, city buildings, farm animals. Parody for anything copyrighted, like south of the boarder (Over the Line), disneyworld (Potato World), Buckies (Duckies). Real names for cities and landmarks.  
• Whimsical surprises: boxes to smash through, puddles to splash, a beach ball to bounce along, fireworks.
• Obstacles such as boxes and rocks are drive-through. Contact triggers a reaction, never a stop or penalty. 

### Do Nots

Make sure none of the following are in the game:
Fail states, crash damage, "try again"
Score, stars, coins, stickers
Unlocks and progression
Timers, races, best times
Text, voiceover, tutorials
Right answers in packing, route or vehicle
Resource management (fuel, money, inventory)
Steering, lanes, wrong turns
Win screen or ending
Saves, profiles, meta-progression
Difficulty settings or age modes
